Dani Fernández is going on tour with *"La Insurrección Tour 2026"* and there are dates that are prepared from the street. To announce the concert calendar in Spain and Mexico we launched a **poster pasting** campaign on urban media: the tour poster pasted right out in public thoroughfares, with the list of dates in view of anyone passing by.

## A tour with confirmed stops

The poster gathers the complete route of the tour and some of the dates are already locked into the calendar:

- 14/03 – Palau Sant Jordi, Barcelona
- 21/05 – Lunario, CDMX
- Movistar Arena, Madrid, with four unique nights
- And many more cities to be announced

With that volume of dates, the challenge was clear: that the public know the tour is coming near their home and that tickets are on sale.

## A poster designed for the street

The design plays with the artist's name in red on a light background, the stamp of *"La Insurrección Tour 2026"* and a list of cities and venues that takes up a good part of the paper. It is a format that is read at a glance and that withstands the elements well, something key when the medium is street furniture and the poster is pasted at the edge of the sidewalk, where there is foot traffic at any hour.

The pasting was done with the usual care: clean surface, paper well tensioned and without bubbles, and a later check to verify that everything is still in place. A wrinkled or peeling poster conveys the opposite of what a tour is looking for: the desire to go.

## Why wild posting fits a tour

**Street marketing** works here for several reasons:

1. Impact on daily life: people see it on the way to work, to the bar or home.
2. Repetition: the same poster at different points in the city reinforces the message.
3. Closeness: it reaches those who do not follow the artist on social media.
4. No filters: it does not depend on the algorithm or on an ad that can be skipped.

Moreover, it is a formula that adapts to any budget and to any phase of the campaign: from the initial announcement to the final push before the concert. For a tour with stops in Barcelona, Madrid, Mexico City and many more cities, having a presence on the street adds up when the public decides to buy a ticket.

If you have a tour, a festival or an event and you want it to be seen on the street, poster pasting is still one of the most direct ways to reach your audience.
